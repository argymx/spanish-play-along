    Using your own passage, or a native speaker's voice
    
    
      
    Under step 1, open Use your own material instead. Drop in a Word file, a PDF or a text file, or paste Spanish straight into the box. The text is read in your browser and never uploaded. It is split into phrases at the punctuation and the sounds in each phrase are tagged automatically, so the advice works on your material too.
    
      
    The model voice is whichever Spanish voice is installed on the device. To use a real person instead, open a phrase's ⋯ menu and choose Use my own model audio. Exam board recordings and a colleague's phone recording both work. Attached audio can be slowed down without changing pitch, so a slowed recording still sounds like someone speaking slowly.
    
    
    
  
    
  
    
    If recording does not work
    
    
      
    Press Check microphone at the top of the page. It runs a full test and tells you exactly where the problem is.
    
      
    The two usual causes are nothing to do with this page. A page opened straight from disk, with a file:// address, is refused the microphone by Chrome and Safari by design. A page inside an embedded frame is refused too. Loading this site from its normal web address avoids both.
    
      
    Also worth checking: whether the device has a microphone at all, since a desktop Mac usually does not, and whether the browser is switched on under System Settings, Privacy and Security, Microphone.
    
      
    You can always record in a voice recorder app and attach the clip from a phrase's ⋯ menu. That route needs no permissions anywhere.
    
    
    
  
    
  
    
    For teachers: what this does and does not do
    
    
      
    This is rehearsal software, not assessment software. It gives a model, a controllable tempo, a silence to speak into, unlimited repetitions and an immediate comparison. It never scores pronunciation, because automated pronunciation scoring is least reliable on exactly the features that matter most here, and a wrong score teaches the wrong thing.
    
      
    The only measured feedback is timing: whether sound was present, when it started and how long it lasted. That cannot be wrong. Everything about the sounds themselves is a self check: the pupil listens, ticks what did not match, and gets a specific explanation and a drill for it.
    
      
    On data protection there is nothing to assess. No audio, text or recording leaves the device, nothing is stored, there are no accounts and no analytics. Recordings live in the browser tab's memory and are gone when the tab closes.
    
      
    Each set has its own link, so you can set one passage or one drill as homework. Use Copy link to this set.
